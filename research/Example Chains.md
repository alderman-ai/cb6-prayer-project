# Example Chains — Reference Research

Real-world fundraising chains of the shape we are building: **Email → Brochure/one-pager (with link or QR) → Landing page → Donation CTA**.

Collected 2026-07-26 for the CB Dejvice sanctuary campaign (54M CZK project, 40M CZK remaining, warm US-based Christian audience).

## How to read this file

Five primary examples below, then two shorter supporting references. Religious examples are weighted most heavily for **tone and word choice**; non-religious examples are here for **structure, progress display, and donation-page mechanics**.

**Honest limitation up front:** email creative is almost never public. None of these organisations publish their appeal emails, so the "Email" link in every chain below is inferred from the landing page's own framing and from the campaign documents that the email would have carried. Where I say something about the email step, it is reconstruction, not a retrieved asset — treat brochure/landing/donate observations as evidence and email observations as inference.

## 1. Cornerstone Church of Lakewood Ranch — "RENEW Sanctuary Project" (religious — closest structural analog)

**Why this one matters most:** a congregation building a new sanctuary, publishing cost, running a multi-year commitment ask, and shipping an actual PDF brochure that hands off to a hosted donation form. This is our chain, already built.

**Assets**
- Landing page: https://www.cornerstonelwr.org/renew-sanctuary-project
- Campaign brochure (PDF, booklet form): https://storage2.snappages.site/VGSRR6/assets/files/BROCHURE-FINAL-PDF-booklet-form.pdf
- Family Campaign Guide (PDF): https://storage2.snappages.site/VGSRR6/assets/files/Family-Campaign-Guide-6x9.pdf
- 21-Day Devotional (PDF): https://storage2.snappages.site/VGSRR6/assets/files/Devotional-for-RENEW-Campaign-2023.pdf
- Donation form (Blackbaud RE NXT, hosted off-site): https://host.nxt.blackbaud.com/donor-form/?svcid=renxt&formId=a469b4d2-92e0-41d3-be9c-e59f5826ff41

**What each step does**
- **Brochure:** the emotional and theological case, plus renderings. Carries the argument; does not carry the transaction.
- **Landing page:** the operational hub. Headline "Renewing our home and renewing our mission." Three named priorities (500-seat sanctuary; convert current worship space to a youth centre; 2,000 sq ft mezzanine), a stated cost ("upward of $7 million"), architectural renderings with the architect and general contractor credited by name, a dated construction-progress update, an FAQ, and four giving rails (online, cheque via the PCA Foundation with a fund number, appreciated stock with a phone number, non-cash assets).
- **Donation CTA:** single online giving button to a third-party hosted form.

**Handoffs**
- PDFs sit *on* the landing page as downloads — the landing page is the hub, the brochure is a leaf, not the other way round. Worth noting: their brochure is a distributable that also lives on the site, so one artefact serves both the mailed/printed path and the web path.
- Landing page → donation form is an off-site hop to Blackbaud. Clean, but it does break visual continuity.

**Tone notes**
- Theological seriousness rather than urgency. They justify spending money on architecture: *"Church design historically reflected the theology of the church"*; *"good architecture has the power to facilitate worship."*
- Borrows an outside authority to make the building argument: *"We shape our buildings and afterwards they shape us."*
- The ecclesiological warrant: *"The church is God's Plan A for the world and that he has no plan B."*
- The FAQ does the defensive work — cost efficiency, why now, why not pay down debt first, how new members participate — which keeps the main page from sounding apologetic.

**Imitate**
- Naming the architect and contractor. It converts "we want to build" into "this is a real project already underway."
- The FAQ absorbing every objection so the persuasive copy can stay warm.
- Explicitly answering "why is this so expensive?" — we have the *same* problem in a sharper form (underground construction driven by Prague 6 regulations). Cornerstone's move is to reframe cost as theology; ours should reframe cost as constraint plus provision, which the one-pager text already gestures at.
- Multiple giving rails listed plainly, including stock and non-cash.

**Avoid**
- The landing page is dense and update-heavy — it reads like an internal congregational hub, not a page a warm outside donor lands on cold from a QR scan. For an external US audience, our landing page needs a shorter, self-contained top section before any of the project-management detail.
- Off-site Blackbaud form with a raw GUID URL. If we can keep the donation on our own domain, or at least on a branded processor page, do it.

## 2. University Presbyterian Church, Chapel Hill — "Foundations of Faith" (religious — transparency under bad news)

**Assets**
- Landing page: https://upcch.org/give/capital-campaign/
- Full campaign details (Google Doc): https://docs.google.com/document/d/1P0sFI-td_XNho2qjoNX6ZP2J9dyjtSxYKBiOCKfGTe0/edit
- FAQ (Google Doc): https://docs.google.com/document/d/1rHcpv3CBL0ACKDS9q1--_dpKcGtTU33hKHdEo1D93WU/edit
- Projects explainer (Google Doc): https://docs.google.com/document/d/1G6wf48Vo9CDJ8UsPm_76mr_ApaCe6crv82-dCqDlPC8/edit
- Feasibility study executive summary (PDF): https://upcch.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/UPC-Campaign-Readiness-and-Feasibility-Executive-Summary.pdf
- Pledge CTA: https://onrealm.org/upcch/AddPledge/foundationsoffaith
- General give CTA: https://onrealm.org/upcch/give

**What each step does**
- **Landing page:** organised as a narrative arc rather than a feature list — "Stewarding our Church Home" → "Welcoming All" → "Loving our Neighbors" → "The Path Forward" → "A Little Background" → timeline → named campaign leadership.
- **Documents:** the substance is pushed into linked docs, including a *published feasibility study*. That is unusually transparent.
- **CTA:** a dedicated named pledge form (`AddPledge/foundationsoffaith`) distinct from the general giving form.

**Handoffs**
- Landing page → Realm pledge form, with the campaign name in the URL path. The pledge is tracked against the campaign, not dumped into general giving. This is the mechanic our donation CTA needs: **a campaign-specific destination, not the church's general giving page.**

**Tone notes**
- Publishes a cost overrun without spin: *"When our HVAC contractor pulled out in late 2025, a new vendor's estimate came in $1.6M higher than our campaign was built on."*
- Frames the response as sequencing, not panic — pairing the capital pledge with the annual stewardship pledge so members "consider both pledges at the same time."

**Imitate**
- Separate campaign pledge destination. Non-negotiable for us.
- Publishing the hard number and the hard news. Our audience is warm and in-network; credibility is the scarce good, not excitement.
- Naming campaign leadership. For a foreign church asking US donors, named humans are trust infrastructure.

**Avoid**
- No stated goal figure anywhere on the page. Fatal for a cold-ish read — a donor cannot size their gift. We must lead with 54M / 14M / 40M.
- Case-for-support content living in editable Google Docs. Looks provisional and internal. Ours must be a designed PDF.

## 3. Friends of Notre-Dame de Paris (religious — the cross-border ask, US donors funding a European building)

**Why this one matters:** it is the closest match to our *jurisdictional* problem — a US 501(c)(3) raising American money for a European church building, and having to make an American donor feel ownership of a building they will probably never sit in.

**Assets**
- Landing / home: https://www.friendsofnotredamedeparis.org/
- Donate: https://www.friendsofnotredamedeparis.org/donate/online/
- Sub-campaign example (gargoyle restoration, hosted): https://secure.givelively.org/donate/friends-of-notre-dame-de-paris-inc/help-restore-notre-dame-s-legendary-gargoyles

**What each step does**
- **Landing page:** hero "Support the Revival of NOTRE-DAME DE PARIS", immediately followed by the credentialing sentence — *"the official 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization spearheading international fundraising efforts to restore and rebuild the iconic Notre-Dame Cathedral."*
- Nav is split into learn / progress / give: Discover → Rebuilding → Support → About (leadership and financials).
- **Trust layer:** GuideStar Gold and Candid seals; explicit *"Your donation to Notre Dame is tax-deductible to the fullest extent allowed by US tax law."*
- **Sub-campaigns:** narrow, nameable, emotionally concrete asks (the gargoyles) alongside the big general fund.

**Handoffs**
- Home → "Donate Now" → own-domain donation page. Sub-campaigns hand off to GiveLively. Newsletter signup captures anyone not ready to give — the second-best conversion.

**Tone notes**
- The ownership move, verbatim: *"Notre-Dame Cathedral is not just a Parisian or a French monument. She belongs to the world, and she needs our help."* That single sentence solves "why should an American care about a building in Europe."

**Imitate**
- **The ownership sentence.** We need our own version: Prague 6 as a mission field the American reader has a stake in, not a foreign building project. The one-pager's "109,000 residents and 38,000 university students" is the raw material — the students in particular make it a *generational* and *European* stake, not a local one.
- Front-loading the legal/tax credential. A US donor giving to a Czech church will ask "is this deductible?" before they ask anything else. Answer it above the fold, on both the one-pager and the landing page.
- Third-party transparency seals or, failing that, named leadership and audited numbers.
- A newsletter/updates capture as the fallback CTA for the not-yet-ready.

**Avoid**
- No downloadable one-pager or brochure anywhere. They rely entirely on site depth — which works for a globally famous cathedral and will not work for an unknown 150-member congregation. Our brochure is doing the recognition work their brand does for free.
- Heritage-preservation framing ("she belongs to the world") is available to them and mostly not to us. Our equivalent lever is mission and secularism, not architecture.

## 4. American University — "Change Can't Wait" (non-religious — landing page structure and progress display)

**Assets**
- Campaign landing page: https://www.american.edu/changecantwait/

**What it does**
- Hero states the number first: **"$500,000,000 Goal Reached"**, then the identity line *"At AU, changemaking is in our DNA."*
- Three named pillars with imagery, description, and a featured story each: **Thrive** (student opportunity), **Inspire** (faculty and research), **Lead** (community and civic impact).
- Allocation shown per pillar ($140M+ / $170M+ / $190M+).
- Participation shown as headcount, not just money: 24,000 alumni, 9,000 faculty and staff, 12,000 students and families.

**Handoffs**
- Weakest link in this example: no prominent, clearly labelled donate destination on the campaign page itself. Structure is excellent; conversion path is not.

**Tone notes**
- Collective, not transactional: *"Together, AU changemakers turned ambition into action. Together, the AU community has given over $500 million dollars."*

**Imitate**
- **Three named pillars.** Our one-pager currently runs a five-bullet benefits list. Compressing to three named pillars (e.g. a permanent home for worship / a visible open door to a secular neighbourhood / capacity for the next generation of students and families) will travel far better across email → brochure → landing page, because a three-part structure can repeat verbatim at every step and build recognition.
- **Participation counts alongside money.** "150 members have committed 14M CZK" is a participation proof point, and it is our single strongest one — it says the people closest to the project have already paid. Give it its own display, not a line in a cost table.
- Number-first hero. Ours: 54M CZK project / 14M committed / 40M remaining.

**Avoid**
- Burying the CTA. Every asset in our chain must have exactly one obvious next action.
- Institutional abstraction ("changemaking is in our DNA"). Inappropriate for a church asking humbly, and empty regardless.

## 5. charity: water — donation page mechanics (non-religious — the last mile)

**Assets**
- Donate page: https://www.charitywater.org/donate

**What it does**
- Headline is the mission, not the transaction: "Help bring clean and safe water to every person on the planet."
- Objection-handling headline right on the donation page: **"You deserve to give with confidence."**
- The trust guarantee, verbatim: *"All of our operational expenses are funded by a private community of donors, so you can trust 100% of your donation will go directly to water solutions, every cent, every time."*
- Four third-party trust marks: Candid Platinum, Charity Navigator 4-star, CharityWatch top-rated, BBB Accredited.
- Multiple giving pathways rather than one form: monthly programme (The Spring), major-gift sponsorship (a stated $10,000+ tier), one-time, plus crypto, cheque, stock, and wire.
- Support contact and help centre visible on the page.

**Handoffs**
- This is the terminal step done well: the page assumes the donor arrived warm from an email or a scan and spends its space on *confidence*, not on re-selling the cause.

**Imitate**
- Put the trust argument on the donation page itself, not only upstream. For us that means: how the money reaches Prague, who holds it, whether it is tax-deductible, and what happens if the project changes scope.
- A stated major-gift tier. charity: water names $10,000+. Our audience includes partner churches and foundations; naming a leadership-gift level gives large donors permission and an anchor. Our one-pager already names the audience ("friends, partner churches, foundations, and individual donors") without giving each of them a corresponding action — that gap should close.
- Multiple rails, especially wire and stock. Cross-border giving from the US will need an explicit mechanism; do not make a foundation guess.

**Avoid**
- Their page offers so many pathways that there is no single default. For a one-time capital campaign, one primary amount-entry path plus a small "other ways to give" block is the right ratio.
- The "100% of your donation" guarantee is a structural promise we cannot make and should not imitate. Substitute concrete transparency (published cost breakdown, named oversight) for an absolute claim.

## Supporting references

### St. John's Lutheran Church, Chaska — "Immeasurably More" (religious — good brochure, broken chain)

- Landing page: https://stjohnschaska.org/campaign/
- Campaign brochure (PDF): https://drive.google.com/file/d/1YOop5acL7NKLFiEfp7LpG3mrlmfOXwza/view
- Newsletter Vol. 1: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1W9Jpbvj_vJqH6nflX4yS-p2lSRTpQffb/view

Useful for two things. First, **tiered goals**: $1.6M already reserved, then a Minimum Goal of $3M ("completion with a mortgage"), a Challenge Goal of $3.5M ("smaller mortgage"), and an Aspirational Goal of $4M ("potentially no mortgage"), with a stated debt policy (under $1M, max five-year term). Each tier is tied to a *consequence the donor can picture*. That is a strong pattern for our 40M gap — a marginal gift needs a visible marginal effect.

Second, the generational argument: *"Just as past generations blessed us with a place to worship and train disciples,"* the current congregation carries it forward. This maps almost exactly onto our founding-in-1995, post-communism story and our own "Building for the Next Generation" line.

**Avoid, emphatically:** the page has **no hyperlinked giving button**. It asks visitors to phone or email the church office. That is a chain that terminates in a dead end — the exact failure our brochure QR code exists to prevent. Also: brochure hosted on Google Drive, which forces a viewer interstitial and looks unprofessional to an external donor. Host our PDF on our own domain.

### NOW Church — "Heart for the House" (religious — giving tiers and multi-rail giving)

- Landing page: https://www.nowchurchfl.com/building
- Give CTA: https://the-now-church-128070.churchcenter.com/giving
- Pledge CTA: https://onrealm.org/TheNowChurch/AddPledge/buildingfund2023

$4,000,000 goal against ~$400,000 already saved. Sections run: The Mission → From the Pastor → Donation Levels → Our Goal → vision → More Ways to Give.

Useful for **named giving tiers with an implied recruitment target**: "Endowed NOW Members" at $10,000+ (seeking 100 supporters), "5-5-5 People of Impact" at $5,000+ (seeking 55), "Golden Donors" at $1,000+, plus an open Other Amount with monthly options. Naming both the tier and the number of people needed turns an abstract goal into a countable, joinable slot. For 40M CZK / ~$1.89M this arithmetic is directly transferable and worth modelling.

Also useful: a distinct **"From the Pastor"** section — the personal-voice block that our email will be written in, mirrored on the landing page so the email's voice and the page's voice match on arrival.

**Tone caution:** *"Our prayer is that our building can become a beacon of God's light"* is the register we want; the surrounding copy ("excited about expanding our reach", CashApp handles) is more promotional than fits CB Dejvice. Take the tier structure, leave the energy.

## Cross-cutting conclusions for CB Dejvice

**On chain mechanics**
1. The landing page should be the hub and the brochure a downloadable leaf from it — but the brochure must also stand alone, because it will arrive by email attachment and by print with a QR code. One artefact, two entry paths (Cornerstone).
2. The donation destination must be **campaign-specific**, not general giving (UPC Chapel Hill's `AddPledge/foundationsoffaith` pattern). A donor who arrives from our QR code and lands on a generic church giving form will assume their money is going into the general fund.
3. Host the PDF on our own domain. Not Google Drive (St. John's), not a raw file-storage URL.
4. Provide a fallback CTA for the not-yet-ready — updates signup (Friends of Notre-Dame). At a $1.89M target, a warm contact who is not ready today is worth capturing.
5. Give the QR code its own tracked URL so scan-sourced traffic is measurable separately from email clicks.

**On what our chain must carry that these mostly don't**
- **Tax-deductibility and the transfer mechanism**, above the fold. Only Friends of Notre-Dame handles this, and it is the single largest unanswered question for a US donor giving to a Czech church.
- **Why underground construction costs what it costs.** Cornerstone's model — meet the cost objection head-on and reframe it — is the template. Our version is regulatory constraint, not extravagance.
- **A named leadership-gift tier** with a headcount attached (NOW Church arithmetic), because partner churches and foundations are explicitly in our audience and currently have no sized action to take.

**On tone**
- The strongest religious examples argue from *stewardship and generational continuity*, not from urgency or scarcity. "Just as past generations blessed us with a place to worship" (St. John's) is the register.
- Transparency reads as humility. Publishing a hard number, a cost problem, and named leaders (UPC Chapel Hill) does more for credibility than any adjective.
- The ownership sentence is the highest-leverage sentence in a cross-border appeal (Friends of Notre-Dame). We need ours and it should appear, near-identically, in the email, the brochure, and the landing page hero.
- Repeat the same three named pillars verbatim at every step of the chain (American University). Recognition across assets is what makes a chain feel like one campaign rather than four documents.
