# US Tax Deductibility — Gifts to CB Dejvice (Czech church, no known US 501(c)(3))

**Research date:** 2026-07-26
**Question:** Can a US taxpayer deduct a gift to CB Dejvice's sanctuary project?
**Short answer:** **No — not for a direct gift**, under current law as of this date. A deduction is only available if the money passes through a US-qualified organisation that has genuine control over the funds.

Everything under "Established fact" is sourced. Everything under "Recommendations" is judgement, not law.

---

## 1. Established fact

### 1.1 The domestic-organisation rule

IRC §170(c)(2)(A) requires that a deductible charitable contribution go to an organisation "created or organized in the United States or in any possession thereof, or under the law of the United States, any state, the District of Columbia, or any possession of the United States."

The IRS states it plainly on its live guidance page (last reviewed/updated **28-Jun-2026**, i.e. current):

> "Except as indicated above, contributions to a foreign organization are not deductible."

— IRS, *Charitable Contribution Deductions*, https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contribution-deductions

IRS **Publication 526** (current edition: 2025, for use preparing 2025 returns) lists among organisations you cannot deduct contributions to:

> "Foreign organizations other than certain Canadian, Israeli, or Mexican charitable organizations."

— IRS Pub. 526, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526

The same page notes that a church or religious organisation *is* a qualifying recipient type — but the domestic-formation test applies on top of that. A Czech church is a religious organisation, but it is not a US organisation, so it fails the test. Being a church does not create an exception.

### 1.2 The treaty exceptions do not include the Czech Republic

The only treaty-based carve-outs are **Canada, Mexico and Israel**, and each is narrow (Canada and Mexico require the donor to have income sourced in that country; all three require the foreign charity to be the equivalent of a US public charity). The US–Czech Republic income tax treaty contains no charitable-contribution article of this kind; the Czech Republic is not among the three countries the IRS names.

- IRS Pub. 526 (Canadian/Israeli/Mexican provisions): https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526
- IRS, Czech Republic tax treaty documents: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/czech-republic-tax-treaty-documents

### 1.3 Foreign activity by a US charity is fine — foreign *recipients* are not

The rule restricts where the recipient is organised, not where the money is spent. A US 501(c)(3) may fund overseas work — including missionary work and buildings abroad — and its US donors keep their deduction, **provided the US organisation retains full control and discretion over the use of the funds.**

— IRS, *Foreign Activities of Domestic Charities and Foreign Charities* (EO CPE text): https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/eotopick92.pdf

This is the mechanism that makes every other option below work.

### 1.4 The "mere conduit" doctrine

If a US organisation exists only to pass earmarked money to a named foreign body at that body's direction, the IRS treats it as a conduit: the gift is recharacterised as a direct gift to the foreign organisation, the deduction fails, and the US organisation risks its exemption. Control must be real — the US board decides timing, amount and approval; the foreign recipient reports back on use of funds.

- Wolters Kluwer, *Deductions for foreign charitable giving*: https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/deductions-for-foreign-charitable-giving
- Venable LLP, *Charity Abroad: U.S. Donors' Options for International Philanthropy*: https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2019/07/charity-abroad-us-donors-options-for-international

Practical consequence for copy: **we must never promise that a gift "designated for CB Dejvice" is deductible.** At most, a gift to a qualifying US organisation whose mission covers this work is deductible, with the designation treated as a non-binding preference.

## 2. Variation by donation method

| Method | Deductible to the US donor? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct wire / card / bank transfer to CB Dejvice in CZK** | **No.** | §170(c)(2)(A); IRS: "contributions to a foreign organization are not deductible." |
| **Gift to a US "Friends of" 501(c)(3)** that grants to the project | **Yes**, if the US entity has genuine discretion and control and is not a conduit. | See 1.3 / 1.4 |
| **Gift to an existing US mission agency / denominational fund** whose purposes cover this work | **Yes**, on the same terms. Usually the fastest route — no new entity needed. | Same |
| **Donor-advised fund (DAF) grant** | The donor's **deduction happened when they funded the DAF** (to the US sponsor). Whether the sponsor will then grant abroad is a separate operational question. | See below |
| **Employer matching gift** | Depends entirely on the employer's program rules, not on tax law. | See below |

**Donor-advised funds.** A DAF donor already took their deduction when they contributed to the sponsoring US charity; recommending a grant to CB Dejvice does not generate a second deduction. Many large sponsors *will* grant internationally, but only after either an **equivalency determination** (a qualified practitioner's good-faith opinion that the foreign body is the equivalent of a US public charity) or **expenditure responsibility** (written grant agreement, pre-grant inquiry, restricted use, reporting back, IRS reporting). These add cost, paperwork and lead time; some sponsors decline foreign grants outright.
— National Philanthropic Trust, *International Grantmaking from a Donor-Advised Fund*: https://www.nptrust.org/philanthropic-resources/philanthropist/the-fundamentals-of-international-grantmaking-from-a-donor-advised-fund/
— Council on Foundations, *International Grantmaking from Donor Advised Funds*: https://cof.org/content/international-grantmaking-donor-advised-funds

**Employer matching.** Most corporate programs restrict matches to US-formed 501(c)(3) public charities. Some large programs (e.g. Google's) will match international organisations, but only after a third-party vendor certifies US-charity equivalency. Assume a direct Czech gift is unmatchable unless the specific employer says otherwise.
— Double the Donation, *Matching Gift Eligibility*: https://doublethedonation.com/matching-gift-nonprofit-eligibility/

**Note on the "Friends of" route:** a US organisation must be genuinely independent — grants made "solely within the exclusive power and absolute discretion" of the US board, never at the foreign organisation's direction.
— Hurwit & Associates, *U.S. "Friends" Organizations for Foreign Charities & NGOs*: https://www.hurwitassociates.com/international-philanthropy-ngos/legal-responsibilities-of-us-quot-friends-quot-organizations/

## 3. Currency check (as of 2026-07-26)

- IRS *Charitable Contribution Deductions* page: **last reviewed/updated 28-Jun-2026** — current, not superseded.
- IRS **Publication 526 (2025)** is the most recent published edition (2026-return edition not yet issued at time of writing). Its foreign-organisation rule is statutory (§170(c)(2)(A)) and was not changed by the 2025 tax act; the 2026 edition is expected to restate it.
- The domestic-organisation requirement has not been amended.

### Changes now in effect for tax year 2026 (relevant to *any* US charitable gift, not to this question specifically)

Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), beginning with tax year 2026:
- **0.5%-of-AGI floor** on itemised charitable deductions — the first 0.5% of AGI in giving is not deductible. Permanent.
- **Non-itemiser deduction** of up to **$1,000 ($2,000 married filing jointly)** for cash gifts to qualifying operating charities (not DAFs).
- **Top-bracket benefit capped at 35%** for itemised deductions, including charitable ones.

Sources: Tax Foundation, *Changes to Charitable Giving Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act*: https://taxfoundation.org/blog/charitable-deduction-big-beautiful-bill/ · Fidelity Charitable, *One Big Beautiful Bill: Impact on Charitable Giving*: https://www.fidelitycharitable.org/articles/obbb-tax-reform.html

These all reduce or complicate the value of a US deduction; none of them create one for foreign gifts.

### Announced for 2027

The only charitable-adjacent change scheduled to take effect in 2027 is the new **nonrefundable credit of up to $1,700** for contributions to **US K-12 scholarship-granting organizations**. It is US-only and irrelevant to this project. **No announced change makes gifts to foreign churches deductible in 2027.**

## 4. Secondary: UK and Ireland (client asked; lower confidence, one source each)

**United Kingdom — no relief.** Gift Aid and other UK charitable reliefs were restricted to UK charities. From **6 April 2024**, charities in the EU/EEA are no longer eligible; only bodies within the jurisdiction of the courts of England & Wales, Northern Ireland or Scotland qualify. A UK donor giving directly to a Czech church gets no Gift Aid and no higher-rate relief.
— HMRC/HM Treasury, *Restriction of charitable reliefs to UK charities*: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/restriction-of-charitable-reliefs-to-uk-charities-and-community-amateur-sports-clubs/restriction-of-charitable-reliefs-to-uk-charities

**Ireland — possible, but requires an application by the church.** Ireland's Charitable Donation Scheme (relief at 31% on donations of €250+, claimed **by the charity**, not the donor) is open to charities established in another EEA/EFTA state or the UK, provided they have obtained a notice of determination from Revenue under s.208A TCA. The Czech Republic is in the EEA, so CB Dejvice is *eligible to apply* — but nothing is available until Revenue grants that determination.
— Irish Revenue, *Charitable Donation Scheme / What is an approved body?*: https://www.revenue.ie/en/companies-and-charities/charities-and-sports-bodies/charitable-donation-scheme/what-is-an-approved-body.aspx and *Non-resident charities*: https://www.revenue.ie/en/companies-and-charities/charities-and-sports-bodies/non-resident-charities/index.aspx

Treat both as directional; if UK or Irish donors become a real segment, get local advice.

## 5. Recommendations (judgement, not law — operator's call)

1. **Default copy position:** state plainly that gifts made directly to CB Dejvice are **not tax-deductible in the United States**. Saying so builds credibility with a warm, church-literate audience, and the alternative — vagueness — invites a donor's accountant to kill the gift later.
2. **Suggested wording pattern (needs client sign-off, not legal advice):** "Gifts made directly to CB Dejvice are not tax-deductible for US taxpayers. If deductibility matters to you, please contact us — we can discuss options." This is accurate, non-promissory, and opens a conversation rather than closing the door.
3. **Highest-leverage fix before launch:** ask the client whether any **existing** US 501(c)(3) — a partner church's missions fund, a denominational mission agency, or a body already supporting Czech ministry — is willing to receive and grant these funds under its own control. That converts "not deductible" into "deductible via X" for the whole US audience at near-zero cost, and is far faster than incorporating a new "Friends of" entity.
4. **Do not** suggest donors "designate" a gift to CB Dejvice through a US charity as if that guarantees a deduction — that is precisely the conduit fact pattern.
5. **Flag for the client:** if a US intermediary is found, the landing page and brochure both need a wording pass, and the intermediary's own counsel should approve the deductibility sentence.
6. Nothing here is tax advice to any individual donor. The copy should carry a short line advising donors to consult their own tax adviser.
