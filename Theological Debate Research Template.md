# Research Purpose and Scope

## Purpose

This is a template for researching at intra- or inter-theological debate on topics pertaining to -- in part or in whole -- the Christian faith. The level of writing and rigor of research must be appropriate to a pastor and graduate student of theology.

Specifically, this template is for researching areas of contention or high-level disagreement and summarizing the era, participants, and neutrally framed arguments of each key participant in the debate.

Once completed, this template should provide as accurate as possible understanding of publicly released information about this topic, including multiple viewpoints, with comparisons between the viewpoints highlighting the core disagreements and whether their validity is supported by direct evidence.

# Frontheader YAML Schema

Category, Type, and Operator name are invariant.  The assistant fills in the remainder according to the constraints and field definitions below.

## Formatting constraints (apply to every field)

- Every field is a single line: `Field Name: value`. Never use block sequences, hyphenated list items, indentation, nested keys, or bracketed flow sequences. This is a hybrid document and the frontheader must remain flat.
- Each value holds 1–4 items separated by commas. Research Date holds exactly one.
- Each item is a noun phrase under 8 words. No sentences, no clauses, no colons, and no commas inside an item — a comma is the item separator and nothing else. Put years in parentheses, e.g. `Great Schism (1054)`, never `Great Schism, 1054`.
- Capitalize the first item and any proper nouns; leave other items lowercase.
- The frontheader is an index, not a summary. If a field cannot be honestly reduced to 4 short items, list the 4 most significant and carry the remainder into the body of the document.
- Never add a field not specified in this schema, and never omit one. If a field genuinely does not apply, its value is `None`.

### Research Date
Exactly one item. ISO 8601 date of completed output.
Example: `2026-08-24`

### Theological Scope
Which pool of opinions, eras, and regions the research draws from. Short scope labels only — the full scope statement belongs in the Research Purpose and Scope body section, not here.
Examples: `All Abrahamic religions` | `Evangelical, Catholic, Orthodox` | `Central European denominations, 20th century` | `American Southern Baptist, Lutheran`

**IMPORTANT:** If no theological scope is defined, the default is global, mainstream, evangelical Christian denominations, dating back to the first Christian churches.

### Topic of Debate
The core interpretation of scripture or events that mainstream denominations, past or present, have or had major disagreements on. State the point of contention, not the conclusion.
Examples: `Relation of Christ's two natures` | `Timing and mode of baptism` | `Authority of the papacy`

### Theological Nomenclature
The 1–3 most commonly used names for the topic or its surrounding events within the academic and theological community. Do not list variants, translations, or near-synonyms of a name already listed.
Examples: `Monophysite controversy` | `Filioque dispute` | `Antinomian controversy`

### Key Books of Bible
If the disagreement concerns the interpretation of scripture, which books contain that scripture. Order by significance to the debate.

Acceptable answers:
- One or more books of the modern Bible, e.g. `Mark` or `Luke, John`
- One or more books not included in the modern Bible IFF that book's exclusion is the PRIMARY TOPIC OF THE DEBATE, e.g. `Enoch`
- `Old Testament` or `New Testament` IFF the entire testament is the PRIMARY TOPIC OF THE DEBATE. I.e., if the primary book pertaining to the debate is Luke, **DO NOT** also add `New Testament`.

If the debate is not primarily exegetical, the value is `None`.

### Key Bodies of the Church
Exactly 2–3 items. The principal institutional parties to the debate — those between whom the disagreement ran, or those a schism divided. Do not list every body that has commented on the topic.
Examples: `Roman Catholic Church, Lutheran World Federation` | `Church of Rome, Church of Constantinople`

### Era of Debate
When the debate was live. Prefer a date range over a named period; use a named period only where dates would mislead. List more than one item only where the debate has discrete, separated phases.
Examples: `1517–1563` | `4th–5th c.` | `1963–present` | `Patristic period`

### Status of Debate
Where the debate stands as of the Research Date. Where academic, ecumenical, and confessional status diverge, list them as separate items rather than qualifying a single one.
Examples: `Ongoing` | `Resolved at Chalcedon (451)` | `Ongoing, ecumenically settled 1999` | `Dormant since 18th c.`

### Key Dissenters
Surnames only — no first names, no titles, no affiliations. One representative principal per position held, so that the number of items equals the number of principal positions in the debate. Name principals, not their followers, students, or commentators. Full rosters

### Schism
Whether the debate produced a formal division, and if so which. Name the schism with its year in parentheses. Where the debate produced no schism but sits downstream of one, name the antecedent as a second item. Examples: `None` | `Great Schism (1054)` | `Protestant Reformation (1517)` | `None, Reformation (1517) as antecedent`
